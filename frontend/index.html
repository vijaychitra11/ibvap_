Most border footage is watched by no one.

Cameras get installed, then the recordings pile up until someone needs them after the fact. A person can't stare at forty feeds for a full shift and still catch the ten seconds that mattered.

IBVAP sits between your existing camera feeds and the people responsible for the sector. It reads the video continuously, decides what's worth a human's attention, and gives every event a record — so the console shows a handful of real moments, not forty walls of static footage.

Capabilities

One console, six ways of watching.

Each module reads from the same live backend, so a fence alert, a plate match, and a flagged face all land on the same timeline.

Camera network

Registered IP camera nodes in one registry — add, edit, and check the health of every node without a site visit.

Alert log

Every event the system has flagged, newest first, with the footage attached — one timeline instead of forty separate feeds.

Virtual fence

Draw a perimeter on the map instead of stringing wire. Zones and detection thresholds are editable any time, from anywhere.

ANPR & vehicles

Plate numbers and vehicle types read straight out of ordinary road-facing footage — no dedicated ANPR rig required.

Behavioral analytics

Face detection, low-light movement, and suspicious-activity flags, derived from video alone — no dedicated recognition hardware.

Assistant

Ask it what's happening right now. Plain-language answers about cameras, alerts, zones, or overall status.

How it works

Live in an afternoon, not a construction project.

IBVAP is software that runs against the camera network you already have — the rollout is a configuration task, not an install.

01

Connect the cameras

Register the IP camera feeds you already operate. No new lenses, poles, or cabling to run.

02

Draw the zones

Set virtual fence lines and detection thresholds for each sector directly on the map.

03

The AI watches

Video is analyzed continuously for crossings, plates, vehicles, and behavior — around the clock.

04

Operators respond

Flagged events land in the alert log with footage attached, ready for a decision, not a search.

Software where the hardware used to be.

Purpose-built recognition rigs and dedicated ANPR cameras are expensive to deploy and hard to extend. IBVAP gets the same capability out of the video feeds already running.
